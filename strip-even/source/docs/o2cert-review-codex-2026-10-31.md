# Referee report: O2 on [1.87, 2]

Review date: 2026-10-31. Sources were reviewed without editing `o2cert/` or `ref/`; authorized driver outputs were regenerated. Findings below distinguish errors in exposition or reusable verification code from defects in this particular proof. No counterexample to O2 was found. Final execution results and verdict appear at the end.

1. **MINOR — Lemma 5's proof has an incorrect intermediate sum; its conclusion is correct.** `o2cert/LEMMAS.md:57` says “the four E's sum to `4 + 2(r1+r3+r5) − 2(r2+r4)`.” They actually sum to `4+r1+2r3+r5−2r2−2r4`. Adding `m1(r1)+m1(r5)=2+r1+r5` gives `6−2σ`, and therefore precisely the identity stated at line 55. Replace the intermediate sum, not the lemma. Independent 60-digit evaluations of the stated identity agree to below `7e-60`.

2. **MINOR — Lemma 1's stated generality exceeds its written proof.** `o2cert/LEMMAS.md:15–17` allows every compact convex set, but the dichotomy “line meets the interior” / “line is top or bottom” omits lower-dimensional sets whose horizontal slice is not extremal. The support formula is nevertheless true. Let `f(y)=max{x:(x,y)∈K}` on the projection of K. On the interior of a nondegenerate projection, this finite concave function has a supergradient `p`; `r=−p` gives equality in the support formula. At a projection endpoint use the stated infinite-slope limit, justified by compactness: any maximizing sequence as `r→+∞` has limit on the top face, with x at most `f(y0)`, while evaluating at `(f(y0),y0)` gives the opposite inequality. A singleton projection is immediate. Add this argument or restrict the lemma to full-dimensional K, which includes every square sum used here. Finite attainment need not hold for an arbitrary convex set at its top/bottom; the lemma correctly uses an infimum. For the polygonal square sums, a sufficiently large finite supporting slope also suffices.

3. **MINOR — Correct the corner proof's dropped factor.** `ref/m1-corner/PROOF.md`, §5, T2, prints `lam(x)+1/(2Rx)=(H Rx−2)/(2xRx)=H/2−1/Rx`. The last expression must be `(H/2−1/Rx)/x`. Multiplication by `W=x(1/x−u)` cancels this factor, so the following displayed factorization `B_X−A(u)=(1/x−u)[A[1/x,u]+H/2−1/Rx]` is correct. `o2cert/src/corner_impl/a7.py:13–26` implements that correct factorization. This is an exposition error, not an invalid A7 scan.

4. **MINOR — The distributed gate command has a stale reference path.** `o2cert/src/driver.py:36–39` opens `../runs/openmath-2026-10-27/m4check.py`, which is absent from this packet. The supplied trusted file is `ref/m4check.py`. My scratch runner changes only the runtime reference loader to that file, preserving exactly the first-29-lines import filter and all gate tests/thresholds. Fix the path or accept an explicit reference-file argument. This affects reproducibility, not the numerical assertion once the specified reference is loaded.

5. **MINOR — Qualify two domain/statistics statements.** `o2cert/LEMMAS.md:87` says “For all r and H” and then applies the `−σ≥5` implication. The affine identity and first inequality hold for all H; the replacement of `−σ` by 5 needs `H≤2`. State the intended `H∈[1.87,2]` restriction in that sentence. Also, the packet's claim that all 186,155 rectangles have positive endpoint margins is inaccurate: 182,455 have numerical margins, and 3,700 have `margin:null, 'sigma'`. 6. **NOTE — Lemmas 1–2 give the required reduction, with no lost tilt or sign case.** At `o2cert/LEMMAS.md:7–29`, the square support is `(|c+rs|+|s−rc|)/2`, and `−r_i dy_i=−s_iHr_i+s_ir_i(h_i+h_{i+1})`. 7. **NOTE — Lemma 3's finite candidate reduction is valid.** `o2cert/LEMMAS.md:35–41`: the two vertex inequalities sum to `φ≥0`; on a sign-constant interval `φ''=−φ≤0`, so its minimum lies at an endpoint. The four listed kink conditions are exhaustive. Both tilt endpoints give `1+(|x|+x)/2+(|y|−y)/2=1+x⁺+y⁻`. For m1, the endpoint value is `1+x⁺`; the negative-x kink gives `1/√(1+x²)`. Extra candidates can only reduce a minimum lower bound, as claimed in part (c); they must not replace or omit an actually available candidate.

8. **NOTE — Lemmas 4, 6 and 7 check algebraically, including the axes.** `o2cert/LEMMAS.md:45–69`: reverse triangle inequality in the two rotated components yields `G(r,t)≥(|r|−1)h(t)`, giving Lemma 4, and reflecting `t↦π/2−t` gives `A(x,y)=A(−y,−x)`. At the positive x kink, the numerator printed in Lemma 6 is correct. For `xY≤1`, rationalizing `E−Kx` gives `x²(x+Y−R)/(R(R+1))`, with Y-slope `x²/(R(R+1))`; for `xY≥1` it gives `1+x+Y−(x+Y)(1+x)/R`, with slope `1−(1+x)/R`. For x>0 the first slope is positive and the second negative. They agree at `Y=1/x`. With `w=x+1/x`, the square identity is `w²(1+2x/(1+x²))=w(w+2)`. Rationalization gives `D=1/(1+w+√(w(w+2)))`, decreasing for `w≥2`. Equality forces x=Y=1. Swapping x,Y gives the other kink. The only candidates in this orthant are E and those two kinks, hence `d=max(0,E−Kx,E−Ky)`. On either axis the deficit is exactly zero, either by direct candidate evaluation or continuity. Evaluating L at π/4 gives `4+8√2−5H`. Also the endpoint value needs no numerical minimization: at H=2, writing `v=sinθ+cosθ` gives `L=2+8/(1+v)`, minimized at `v=√2`, hence `F6(2)=8√2−6`.

9. **NOTE — Lemmas 8–11's slope and clipping bounds are safe at the exact endpoints.** `o2cert/LEMMAS.md:73–91`: for x<0, `∂x(G+xh)` is 0 or sin t; on its nonzero branch `tan t<1/|x|`, so `sin t≤1/√(1+x²)`. For y>0, `∂y(G−yh)` is 0 or −cos t; the latter requires `tan t>y`, so its magnitude has the same bound. At kinks use the interval between the adjacent slopes. A minimum over t inherits uniform local increment bounds: integrate a bound valid for every t, take minima, and use the reverse increment as well. Equivalently use upper/lower Dini derivatives; no unjustified differentiability of the minimum is needed. The derivative of negative-x m1 has maximum `2/(3√3)` at `|x|=1/√2`. The threshold check is strict: `1.87²(1+0.38²)−4=0.00185236>0`, so `2/√1.1444−1.87=−0.0004328388233…`. The endpoint coordinates r1 and r5 have identical `m1+A−Hr` dependence. After clipping, the sum of all right-sign magnitudes is less than `5+3·0.38=6.14` whenever `−σ<5`; the other magnitudes are already bounded by 0.38. Thus q really belongs to K. The strict upper bound causes no gap because K and the stored enclosing intervals are closed.

## Verdict

**Yes: O2 is certified on [1.87,2] under the stated premises.** The analytic reduction is valid with the minor text repairs above; the outward-rounded computation covers the required complement, the corner scans close the excluded product, and every saved rectangle was successfully rechecked. There are no remaining MAJOR findings. This is conditional on the supplied vertex reduction, the identification of F6 with min L and the corner surrogate's symbolic/value/stationarity premises, and on IEEE-754 binary64 behavior with the stipulated eight-ulp libm enclosure premise. I have not independently proved those external premises or the platform libm error bound, nor converted the proof into a proof-assistant derivation. Random gates are supporting diagnostics, not additional premises needed to replace the analytic and exhaustive checks.
